How to playAssisto · five-a-side, snooker rules

The assist

The ball must strike one of your own players before it touches anything else. After that it can do as it likes — carom off a defender, off the boards, into the net. That is the whole game: pass, then finish.

ON: RED tells you whose players are legal to hit, exactly as snooker tells you which ball is on. Legal players wear a bright collar and a target ring; the other side fades back.

Kick-off: you can't score straight from a kick-off. If it goes in, it's a goal kick to them — you still keep your break and your nudge.

Fouls

  • You strike an opponent first.
  • You touch nobody and the ball barely moves — not a real attempt.
  • You run out of time on the shot clock without playing.
  • A goal scored from any of the above is disallowed.

A foul gives your opponent a free shot: for that one shot anything is legal, so they can fire straight at goal. The mouth lights up amber when it is on.

Fouls add up across the match, as they do in futsal — the pips beside your goal are the running total, and your sixth concedes a penalty from the spot. They do not clear on their own, but a break of ten pardons one.

Not fouls

  • A properly struck shot that threads everyone: you simply lose the turn.
  • Ball resting against your own player? You are deemed to have hit it — play away.
  • Ball resting against an opponent? That contact is ignored, not held against you.
  • A kick-off into the net — no goal and no foul. The ball comes out for a goal kick, and your break survives.
  • An own goal always counts, foul or not. Mind your own net.

The nudge

Find your own player and you earn a nudge: that player may move up to two of its own widths. Foul and you get nothing. A player may only move within its own part of the pitch, may not crowd a team-mate or a goalpost so tightly the ball cannot pass, and may not slide up against an opponent or onto the ball. Your keeper can be nudged too, but only along its own goal line. Tap the ringed player and drag, or tap anywhere else to decline.

Playing a friend

Create a match and send the link, or type in their code. You take the bottom goal and they take theirs, so you both play up the screen — the scoreboard reads YOU and THEM from where you are sitting.

The shot clock runs on the match server, not your phone, so both of you see the same countdown however slow the connection is. Pausing does not stop it. The host's choices — time per shot, goals to win, match length — are the ones the match is played on.

Matchday

One fixture a day, the same for everybody: the same board, the same opponent, the same clock. You get one attempt, and where you finish is decided by the result first, then the margin, then your best break, then who needed fewer shots.

Play on consecutive days and a streak builds. Miss one and it starts again. Catching up on a matchday you missed does not move the streak — a streak is days in a row.

Every run on the board is replayed on your own phone from the shots that produced it. A run that reproduces is ranked; one that does not is listed below the table rather than removed, along with runs played on an older version of the game, which cannot be compared because the opponent has changed.

You can start the fixture again if it is going badly, and nothing stops you. The board simply says how many attempts a result took, with a * beside the score. Your result carries the name you have set, and you will be asked for one before your first fixture.

The league

Up to twenty of you, twenty-one matchdays, one table. There is one match a day and everybody plays it — the same board, the same opponent. The table is built from how each of you got on against it, so nobody waits for anybody.

Three points for beating the machine, one for a draw, then goal difference, then goals scored. A new matchday opens each day, and every one already open stays playable: join late and you simply have a few to catch up on.

When the twenty-one are done the season ends, whoever is top wins it, and the table goes in your trophy cabinet. Anyone can start the next season.

A league has a name nobody else can take, and its own code. Leagues set to open are listed for anyone to join; the rest are found by name or code, so only the people you send it to can get in. Whoever starts one decides how many may join, up to twenty.

You can be in as many leagues as you like — one match a day, and it counts in all of them. Leave whenever you want: your seat frees up and your results stay in the tables you played them in. If you are the last one out, the league goes with you and its name is free again.

Trophies

Three things end up in your cabinet, and nothing else in the game is permanent. Finishing a league season in the top three. Finishing a career — every level of the campaign. And finishing a month in the top three of the daily board, counted the same way a league is.

Your progress

Campaign stars, unlocked levels, trophies and your match settings are kept in this browser, so you can close the tab and pick up where you left off. Clearing your browser data clears them. Private browsing usually blocks it, in which case the game still plays but nothing is remembered.

Finish all the campaign levels and you can walk the ladder again on fresh boards. Each time through is a career, and each one finished is a line in the cabinet.

The board

The teams are laid out once per match and stay there. A goal returns the ball to the centre spot and nothing else moves, so the gaps and lanes you learn in the first minute are the ones you will be playing all game.

The break

Every shot that finds your own player extends your break, shown beside the foul pips. A goal extends it too — only a foul, or a shot that touches nobody, ends it. It runs for the whole match, not the round, so a spell of clean shooting survives a goal.

Controls

Drag anywhere and pull back, like a slingshot — the pull is measured from your finger, not from the ball, so you can do it out in open space. Run out of screen? Hold at the edge and the power keeps winding up. The dotted line traces about a third of the pitch, including the first deflection; the rest is yours to read.

Your team

Five outfield players plus a keeper. Your shape guards your own goal and gives you the outlets to attack with, so a heavy defence is safe but short of passing options. Pick it under Your shape when you set up a match. You must keep three in the back two lines — what guards the goal is bodies in the middle, so a lone defender is fine if the midfield is full. Three at the back roughly halves what leaks through the centre.
